### Seeing people

OpenCV and Python detect a person’s head in the camera image and track its position, so the robot can follow it accurately. [Head tracking on video](https://youtu.be/Tbja_HpTQHE)

### Moving like the operator

For teleoperation, a Kinect tracks the operator’s skeleton, and inverse kinematics turns the hand positions into joint angles for the robot, which imitates the operator’s hands. A separate interface controls the movements of the upper body. [Teleoperation on video](https://youtu.be/_JGYox5_IY8) · [Upper-body control on video](https://youtu.be/0VcOwU8_J2A)

### Listening

Speech recognition with hotword detection lets people talk to the robot: it waits for its hotword, then listens. [Speech interaction on video](https://youtu.be/j74jeWoHeEo)

### Being there remotely

With a virtual-reality headset, an operator’s head movements are carried over to the robot’s head, so it can be operated remotely at a distant event. A C#/WPF desktop app controls the robot over the internet through Firebase. [The main demo on video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uJDjwlhpj1g)

### Looking back

This project sits early on the thread that runs through my work: software that connects people and machines. The head tracking from 2019 comes back in HoloBox.
